# Copy

Generated copy is vague, inflated and rhythmically identical. Classy copy is specific, calm and sounds like one person who knows the business.

## Write the copy first

Before layout, write every section as plain text: headline, one or two supporting lines, the action. If the page reads well as a text file, the design will be easy. If it does not, no design will save it.

## Banned words and phrases

The scanner flags these. Replace each with what actually happens.

**Verbs:** elevate, unlock, unleash, supercharge, empower, revolutionize, transform (as filler), harness, leverage, streamline (without saying what), reimagine, skyrocket, turbocharge, delve, embark.

**Adjectives:** seamless, cutting-edge, next-level, world-class, best-in-class, state-of-the-art, game-changing, innovative, robust, powerful (unqualified), effortless, holistic, bespoke (unless it really is), tailored (unless explained), curated (unless someone actually curates).

**Phrases:** "in today's fast-paced world", "in the ever-evolving landscape", "take your X to the next level", "your all-in-one solution", "look no further", "whether you're a X or a Y", "we've got you covered", "say goodbye to X", "it's not just X, it's Y", "the future of X is here", "join thousands of", "designed with you in mind", "a testament to", "rest assured".

**Structures:** triplets of one-word sentences ("Fast. Simple. Secure."), headlines as questions ("Ready to grow?"), stacked rhetorical questions, ending every paragraph on a punchline.

**Punctuation:** no em dashes in copy. Use a comma, a colon, a period or parentheses. Avoid exclamation marks except in genuinely celebratory moments (an order confirmed).

## What good copy does

- **Specific over general.** "Tables for 2 to 40, booked in under a minute" beats "Seamless reservations".
- **Concrete nouns.** Name the dish, the city, the deliverable, the time saved.
- **Real numbers with context.** "Cut prep time from 40 to 25 minutes across 3 kitchens" beats "10x efficiency".
- **The visitor's words.** Use the terms customers use in reviews and support emails.
- **Short sentences, varied length.** Rhythm comes from variation, not from triplets.
- **One idea per sentence.**

## Headlines

Not formulas, but useful shapes:

- **Outcome:** what the visitor gets. "Dinner for twelve, without the phone tag."
- **Identity:** who it is for. "Bookkeeping for Toronto restaurants."
- **Contrast with the status quo:** "Your menu, your prices, no marketplace commission."
- **Plain statement of what it is:** often the classiest choice. "Hand-made pasta. Queen West. Since 2014."

Test: could a competitor use this exact headline? If yes, rewrite.

## Buttons and links

- Verb + object: "Book a table", "See pricing", "Download the menu", "Start free trial".
- One primary action per screen; secondary actions look secondary.
- Link text describes the destination. Never "click here" or a bare "Learn more" repeated five times.

## Microcopy

- Form labels are visible, not just placeholders.
- Errors say what happened and how to fix it: "Card number is 2 digits short", not "Invalid input".
- Empty states say what goes here and offer the first action.
- Loading states are honest; do not fake progress.
- Success messages confirm the specific thing: "Table for 4 booked, Friday 7:30 pm. We sent a confirmation to amy@…".

## Proof

- Testimonials: real name, real role or context, real permission. Short excerpt, not a paragraph.
- Logos: only real clients, with permission.
- Ratings: only with a source.
- If proof does not exist yet, leave a marked placeholder `[REAL QUOTE NEEDED]` and tell the user. Never invent it.

## Tone checks

Read the page aloud. If it sounds like an ad, cut adjectives. If it sounds like a press release, cut nouns. If it sounds like a person who runs the business explaining it to a friend, ship it.
